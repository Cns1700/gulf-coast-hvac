Services

Repair, heating, seasonal tune-ups, and emergency calls. We work on the systems already in your house. We do not quote prices on this site — you get a written diagnosis first.

AC repair

South Alabama cooling season is long. Units run from April into October, and the air is wet. Frozen coils, weak airflow, and short cycling are the calls we get most. We start with gauges, a drain check, and a look at the indoor coil before we talk parts.

  • No cooling, or air that never gets cold enough
  • Ice on the copper lines or the indoor coil
  • Weak airflow from some or all vents
  • Short cycling — the unit starts and stops over and over
  • Standing water at the indoor unit or a wet ceiling nearby

Heating

Winters here are mild until they are not. A two-day cold snap is enough to find a heat pump stuck in defrost or a furnace that will not ignite. We service heat pumps, gas furnaces, and the thermostat and safety switches that sit between you and heat.

  • No heat, or heat that never quite catches up
  • Heat pump running but blowing room-temperature air
  • Uneven rooms — one side of the house stays cold
  • Pilot, igniter, or flame-sensor problems on gas furnaces
  • Strange smells or a unit that shuts down on a safety switch

Tune-ups

A tune-up is a scheduled visit, not a repair. We clean what we can reach, test what fails in this climate, and tell you what is wearing out. Most homes here do well with a spring cooling check and a fall heating check. We will say so if a second visit is not worth it.

  • Clean outdoor coil and check indoor coil condition
  • Test capacitors, contactors, and safety switches
  • Tighten electrical connections and measure amp draw
  • Clear the condensate drain and check the float switch
  • Replace the filter if you have one on site, and set the thermostat

Emergency

Some failures cannot wait for Monday. No cooling in a heat advisory, a gas furnace that will not stay lit, or water coming through a ceiling from a clogged drain — those are emergency calls. Sunday is emergency only. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 first, then us.

  • System completely dead after a storm or a power blink
  • No cooling during high heat, especially with children or elders at home
  • Water leaking from the indoor unit
  • Burning smell from the air handler (shut the system off first)
  • Heat out during a freeze warning
