About the shop

Small Mobile shop. We work on the systems already in the house, stay inside three counties, and write down what we found before we ask you to spend money.

Bayou Road, 1998

Dale Landry spent the 1980s as a shipyard electrician, then went to night school for HVAC. In 1998 he and Miriam opened a two-bay shop on Bayou Road with one van and a used recovery machine. The idea was simple. Mobile already had the big companies. What it needed was people who would tell you a capacitor failed without trying to sell you a whole system.

Down here, cooling season is the job. Salt air eats outdoor coils. Pollen cakes them in April. Afternoon storms flood drain pans. We learned that by living it, not from a training slide. Miriam still answers the phone most weekdays. Dale still rides along on the ugly ones — no cool air in August, or a furnace that will not light on a freeze night.

Two of their kids work here now. The rest of the crew is techs who stayed. We are licensed and insured in Alabama. Not a franchise, no national call center. If you call the shop, someone on Bayou Road picks up, or you get a voicemail we actually listen to.

Three values we actually use

  • What's written is what you see

    The invoice should match what we found. If it is a capacitor, we say that. If the coil is on its way out and a repair is just buying time, we say that too. No extra line items.

  • Coastal knowledge

    Humidity, salt, and pollen are part of the job here. We clean, drain, and treat the unit for Gulf weather — not for a dry-climate textbook.

  • Finish the job

    Getting the air moving again is not the whole visit. We check the drain, the filter, the thermostat, and the outdoor disconnect before we leave the driveway.
